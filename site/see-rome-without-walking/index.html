How to see Rome without walking

Rome is hilly, cobbled and spread out. These are the easiest ways to see it if long walks aren't an option.

Updated October 2026

Quick answer

The easiest way to see Rome without walking is a golf cart tour: in about 3 hours you see 6–8 major sights, mostly from the cart, and private tours usually pick you up at your hotel. Other good options are a Vespa or vintage Fiat 500 tour with a local driver, a private car with driver, or a hop-on hop-off bus, which is cheapest but can't enter the narrow historic streets.

1. Golf cart tour: the easiest overall

Electric carts with a driver-guide can drive into the historic center and stop right next to the Colosseum, the Trevi Fountain area, the Pantheon and Piazza Navona. Most tours last 2.5–3 hours and cover 6–8 sights. Private tours usually include hotel pickup in the center and can carry a folded wheelchair. Read more in our guide for seniors and limited mobility or compare the best golf cart tours.

2. Vespa with a driver

You ride as a passenger behind a professional driver, or in a sidecar. It's more of an experience than a tour, and great for photos. It's less suitable for anyone who finds getting on and off a scooter difficult. See our Vespa tour guide.

3. Vintage Fiat 500 with a driver

A 1960s Fiat 500 with a local driver: iconic, comfortable to sit in, and small enough for the narrow lanes. Cars usually take 2–3 guests. See our Fiat 500 tour guide.

4. Hop-on hop-off bus

The cheapest way to cover a lot of ground, but buses stay on the main roads, so you still walk from the stops to the Pantheon, Trevi and Navona. Fine for an overview, less so if walking is the problem.

Tips for a low-walking day in Rome

  • Book tours with hotel pickup so there's no walk to a meeting point.
  • Go early morning or evening in summer: midday heat makes even short walks hard.
  • For the Colosseum and Vatican, book timed tickets to avoid standing in queues, and ask about accessible routes.
  • Combine one golf cart tour for the overview with one visit inside a monument, rather than many stops.

Questions people ask

What is the easiest way to see Rome without walking?

A golf cart tour: the cart drives up to the main sights, you see 6–8 of them in about 3 hours, and private tours usually include hotel pickup.

Can you see Rome in a wheelchair?

Yes. Many golf cart tours accept a folded manual wheelchair, which takes about two seats. Tell the operator when booking.

Is the hop-on hop-off bus good if I can't walk much?

Partly. It covers a lot, but stays on main roads, so you still need to walk from the stops to sights like the Pantheon and Trevi Fountain.

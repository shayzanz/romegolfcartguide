Golf cart tours with gelato and food

Sightseeing with something to eat along the way: from a simple gelato stop to a full pasta tasting.

Updated October 2026

Quick answer

Several Rome golf cart tours include food: some add a gelato or espresso stop to a 3-hour sightseeing route, while others finish with a tasting of classic Roman pastas in a trattoria. Expect about $99–130 per person on a shared tour and more for private food tours.

Three kinds of food tour

  • Gelato stop: a classic highlights tour with a break at an artisan gelateria. The easiest option for families.
  • Coffee and gelato: private tours that include an espresso and a gelato along the way.
  • Pasta tasting: sightseeing that ends in a trattoria with three Roman classics. Think cacio e pepe, carbonara and amatriciana.

Where the food stops happen

Trastevere is the most popular area: narrow cobbled streets, ivy-covered houses and trattorias with tables outside. Testaccio, the old working-class district by the former slaughterhouse, is where Romans go for traditional cooking and has a covered market. Some tours also stop in the Jewish Ghetto, known for fried artichokes.

Good to know

  • Tell the operator about allergies or a vegetarian diet when you book. Roman pasta is easy to adapt, but they need to know.
  • Evening food tours combine well with Rome lit up. See our night tour guide.
  • Drinks other than water are usually extra unless the listing says otherwise.

Questions people ask

Which Rome golf cart tours include gelato?

Several classic 3-hour tours include a gelato stop, and some private tours include both coffee and gelato. Use the 'Gelato or food stop' filter on our homepage.

Is there a golf cart tour with pasta?

Yes. One popular tour offers a version that ends with a tasting of three classic Roman pasta dishes in a local trattoria.

Can food tours handle allergies?

Usually, if you tell the operator when booking.

One day in Rome: an itinerary built around a golf cart tour

Only one day? Use the cart to see the big sights in the morning, then go inside one of them in the afternoon.

Updated October 2026

Quick answer

To see Rome in one day, take a 3-hour golf cart tour in the morning (around 9–10am) to cover the Colosseum, Circus Maximus, the Aventine keyhole, the Pantheon, Piazza Navona, Trevi and the Spanish Steps. Have lunch in the center, then spend the afternoon inside the Colosseum or the Vatican Museums, and finish with dinner in Trastevere.

Morning: golf cart tour (9:30am–12:30pm)

Start early: traffic is lighter and the Trevi Fountain is less crowded. A classic 3-hour route usually covers:

  • The Colosseum and the Arch of Constantine
  • Circus Maximus and the view of the Palatine Hill
  • The Aventine Hill: the famous keyhole and the Orange Garden viewpoint
  • The Mouth of Truth and the Theatre of Marcellus
  • Piazza Venezia, the Pantheon and Piazza Navona
  • The Trevi Fountain and the Spanish Steps

Ask the guide to drop you near your lunch spot at the end.

Lunch (1pm)

Stay in the center: the streets around the Pantheon and Campo de' Fiori are full of trattorias. Try one of the four Roman pastas: carbonara, cacio e pepe, amatriciana or gricia.

Afternoon: go inside one sight (2:30–5:30pm)

Golf carts don't go inside monuments, so pick one to visit properly, with tickets booked in advance:

  • The Colosseum and Roman Forum for ancient Rome
  • The Vatican Museums, Sistine Chapel and St. Peter's for art
  • The Borghese Gallery for a shorter, quieter visit (booking essential)

Evening: Trastevere

Cross the river to Trastevere for an aperitivo and dinner among ivy-covered streets. In summer, consider swapping the morning for a night golf cart tour instead.

Questions people ask

Can you see Rome in one day?

You can see the highlights. A 3-hour golf cart tour covers 6–8 major sights in the morning, leaving the afternoon to visit one monument inside.

What time should I book the golf cart tour?

Around 9–10am for the lightest traffic and the calmest Trevi Fountain, or an evening tour in summer.

Should I visit the Colosseum or the Vatican?

Ancient history fans should pick the Colosseum and Forum; art lovers the Vatican Museums. Doing both in one afternoon is too rushed.

Golf cart, walking tour or hop-on bus?

An honest comparison of the main ways to see Rome in a few hours.

Updated October 2026

Quick answer

A golf cart tour covers 6–8 major sights in about 3 hours with almost no walking and gets into narrow streets buses can't reach, but costs more (from about $69, typically $100+ per person). A walking tour is cheaper but means 10+ km on cobblestones, and a hop-on bus is cheap but stays on the big roads.

When a golf cart is the best choice

  • You only have one or two days in Rome
  • It's summer and hot
  • You're travelling with kids, grandparents or anyone who can't walk far
  • You want a guide's stories without a long walk

When something else makes more sense

  • Walking tour: you're on a tight budget, love walking, and want to go inside sites like the Colosseum or the Vatican Museums, which carts don't enter.
  • Hop-on bus: you want flexible transport across the whole city over a full day.
  • Vespa: you're a confident rider (or happy riding pillion) and want something more adventurous.

Many visitors do a golf cart tour on day one to get their bearings, then book a guided visit inside the Colosseum or the Vatican on another day.

Questions people ask

Is a golf cart tour better than a walking tour in Rome?

For covering a lot in little time with no effort, yes. For going inside monuments or saving money, a walking tour is better.

Do golf carts go inside the Colosseum?

No. Carts stop outside for photos and stories. You need a separate ticket or guided tour to go inside.

Can golf carts drive in Rome's historic center?

Yes. Licensed tour carts are street-legal and can reach narrow streets in the center where big buses can't go.
